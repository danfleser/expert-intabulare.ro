Three separate bills, one transaction

When a foreign buyer asks "what does registration cost in Romania", the honest answer is that three unrelated parties charge you, and confusing them is how people end up feeling overcharged by the wrong person.

  • The state, through ANCPI/OCPI. ANCPI is the national cadastre and land registration agency; each county has an OCPI office, and within it local BCPI bureaux that actually hold the land books. Their tariffs are set by ministerial order (Order 16/2019, repeatedly amended) — they are public, identical in every county, and not negotiable by anyone.
  • The notary public. A separate liberal profession with its own national fee schedule. Any transfer of ownership, succession, division deed or power of attorney passes through a notary, and that fee has nothing to do with the land registry.
  • The surveyor. Our fee, freely priced, for measuring the property and producing the documentation OCPI accepts. That side is covered on our survey cost guide; this page is deliberately only about the state's share.

Ask any quote you receive to separate these three. A quote that folds them into one number is not necessarily dishonest, but you cannot compare it to anything.

Four things foreign owners consistently get wrong

"There must be a cadastre tax." There used to be. Registering an unregistered property for the first time is currently exempt from the state tariff — a deliberate policy choice to remove the barrier, since roughly a third of Romanian property is still outside the electronic land book. If someone bills you a "state cadastre tax" for a standard first registration, ask which service code in the tariff annex it corresponds to.

"The free extract must be a limited version." It is not. Since 2025, owners obtain the information extract and the cadastral plan extract on orthophoto free of charge, at the counter and then online through MyEterra; hundreds of thousands are issued that way each month. What you pay for is only the authentication extract, and that one exists to freeze the land book for a signing.

"0.15% of what, exactly?" Of the price declared in the notarial deed, not of the market value, not of our fee, and not of the cadastral value. On a purchase, expect the notary to collect it alongside their own fee and remit it when the deed is filed. If the property has never been registered, you are in the exempt first-registration lane instead — a different track, not a discount.

"Fees must be cheaper in a small county." They are national. OCPI Alba, OCPI Cluj and OCPI Sibiu charge the same for the same service. What genuinely differs between offices is workload, and therefore how long a file waits — which is a scheduling question, not a pricing one.

The urgency regime, and the costs that are not registry fees

Most OCPI services have an urgency variant: you pay a supplementary tariff and the processing term falls sharply. It is worth paying whenever a real date depends on it — a signing appointment, a mortgage drawdown, a grant deadline. Two caveats decide whether it is money well spent. First, it buys nothing on a first registration: under the term schedule in force since August 2025, that service carries the same term in the standard and the urgent regime, so paying extra changes only the invoice. Second, it cannot override the priority principle: where earlier applications on the same property are still unresolved, your file waits its turn regardless. Our timeline guide sets out which services the urgency regime genuinely shortens.

Budget separately for items that feel like registry fees but are not: the notarial fee on the deed itself; certified translations and, for documents issued abroad, an apostille; the fiscal certificate from the town hall confirming local taxes are paid (free in many communes, a token charge in others); the inheritance tax of 1% that applies where a succession is settled more than two years after the death; and the urbanism certificate where an operation requires one. None of these reach ANCPI, but all of them reach your bank account, so we list them in every estimate we give.

Frequently asked questions

So first registration really costs nothing to the state?

Yes — the first-registration services are exempt under the current tariff order, so your only cost is the surveyor's fee for producing the documentation. And there is no faster track worth buying here: the processing term for a first registration is the same whether you pay the urgency tariff or not.

Who is legally responsible for paying the OCPI tariffs — me or the surveyor?

The beneficiary of the work, meaning you. In practice we quote them separately and pay them on your behalf when filing, so you see exactly what went to the state and what was our fee.

I am buying a house that already has cadastral registration. What do I owe OCPI?

0.15% of the price in the deed as an individual buyer, with a minimum amount per application, normally collected by the notary at signing. If the property has not physically changed, you do not need a surveyor at all for the transfer.

Can I order an extract myself from abroad?

If you are the registered owner, yes — through ANCPI's online platform, free of charge. If you are a prospective buyer, you or your representative can order an information extract for a small tariff; our guide on ordering a land registry extract walks through it, and we read the result with you if the entries are unclear.

Do these tariffs change?

Periodically, by order — the exemptions described here are themselves recent amendments. We confirm the current figures for your file at the moment of filing rather than relying on any published table, including this one.
