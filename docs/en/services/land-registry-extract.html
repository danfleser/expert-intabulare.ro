What a Romanian land registry extract is

The extras de carte funciară is the official certified copy of a property's land book entry, issued by the county land registration office (OCPI) under the national agency ANCPI. It is the closest Romanian equivalent to a title search report: one document that tells you who owns the property, exactly what the property is, and what claims are attached to it.

Every serious step in a Romanian property transaction runs on this document. Notaries will not authenticate a sale without a fresh one; banks will not lend without it; EU grant authorities typically require one issued within the last 30 days.

How to read it: the three sections

A land book entry has three parts, and each matters:

  • Part I (Foaia A) — the property. Cadastral number, location, surface area, land-use category, and buildings, if any. This is where you check whether the registered area matches what you think you are buying.
  • Part II (Foaia B) — the owners. Who holds title, in what shares, and under which legal act (purchase, inheritance, court decision, restitution title). Co-ownership in undivided shares is common on agricultural land and changes your whole acquisition strategy.
  • Part III (Foaia C) — encumbrances. Mortgages, easements (such as a neighbour's right of way or a power line corridor), usufruct, superficie rights, seizure notes, pending litigation, and sale prohibitions. Anything in this section can delay, complicate or kill a transaction — this is the section foreign buyers most often fail to read.

What we do beyond fetching the document

Obtaining the extract is the easy part — ANCPI even issues informative extracts online. Our value is the reading: we check the extract against the cadastral plan and the national geoportal, verify that the parcel geometry does not overlap a neighbour, compare the deed area with the registered and measured area, and translate the legal notes in Part III into plain English with a clear verdict: safe to proceed, proceed with these conditions, or walk away.

This is a desk service we provide for property anywhere in Romania, usually within days. For land acquisitions and PV projects we bundle it into a fuller land due diligence survey that adds field measurements.

Întrebări frecvente

How current does an extract need to be?

Legally an informative extract has no expiry, but in practice institutions want a recent one — grant authorities and banks commonly require issuance within 30 days. Since the land book can change any day, we always pull a fresh extract before giving an opinion.

Can a foreigner request an extract for a property they don't own?

Yes. The Romanian land book is public: anyone can request an informative extract for any property, which is exactly what makes proper due diligence possible before you commit.

The property has no land book at all. What does that mean?

It means the property has never been through first registration — common for rural land. There is nothing to extract yet; the path forward is cadastral registration. See our page on first registration of unregistered land.
